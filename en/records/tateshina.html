Tateshina

  • TSN-JOMON-001
    Grade S
    Togariishi and Yosukeone Sites (Special Historic Site 'Togariishi Stone Age Site') and Miyasaka Hideichi

    Togariishi is a Middle Jomon settlement on the western foot of the Yatsugatake range, designated a Historic Site in 1942 and a Special Historic Site in 1952. A local schoolteacher, Miyasaka Hideichi, began digging it, largely on his own, in 1930, and the adjoining Yosukeone site was excavated from 1946 to 1952.

    TSN-JOMON-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-JOMON-002
    Grade S
    National Treasure Dogu 'Venus of Jomon' (Tanabatake Site)

    A near-complete Middle Jomon standing figurine, 27 cm high, was unearthed on 8 September 1986 at the Tanabatake site in Chino during rescue excavation ahead of an industrial park. Designated a National Treasure on 15 June 1995 under the name 'Dogu', it is owned by Chino City and kept at the Togariishi Museum of Jomon Archaeology.

    TSN-JOMON-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-JOMON-003
    Grade S
    National Treasure Dogu 'Goddess with a Mask' (Nakappara Site)

    A hollow Late Jomon figurine, 34 cm high, was found on 23 August 2000 at the Nakappara site in Chino, lying on its side in the bottom of Pit 70 in a cluster of pits thought to be graves. Designated an Important Cultural Property in 2006, it became a National Treasure on 21 August 2014 together with eight accompanying vessels.

    TSN-JOMON-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-JOMON-004
    Grade S
    Togariishi Museum of Jomon Archaeology and the 'Togariishi' Stone (Togariishi-sama)

    The museum began as a private exhibition room opened in 1951 by Miyasaka Hideichi, became the town-run Togariishi Archaeological Museum in 1955, moved to a new building in 1982 and was renewed as the Togariishi Museum of Jomon Archaeology in 2000. The site takes its name from a pyramid-shaped boulder on the south slope, described in the 1942 designation as an object of worship.

    TSN-JOMON-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-JOMON-005
    Grade A
    Tsumetayama and Mugikusa Pass obsidian sources and the Japan Heritage story 'The Jomon World of the Central Highlands under Starfall'

    Obsidian outcrops at Tsumetayama in Chino are listed among the city's components of the Japan Heritage story certified on 24 May 2018. X-ray fluorescence studies group obsidian from Tsumetayama, Shibunoyu and Mugikusa Pass in the Tateshina area into one 'Tsumetayama group'.

    TSN-JOMON-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-JOMON-006
    Grade S
    Akyu Site (Hara Village) and its preservation under the Chuo Expressway

    Akyu is an Early Jomon settlement with a ring of stone clusters about 120 m across, found during excavations ahead of the Chuo Expressway in 1975-78. After a preservation movement, road plans were altered, the remains were backfilled and buried under the road, and the site was designated a National Historic Site on 2 July 1979.

    TSN-JOMON-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-HIST-001
    Grade A
    Uehara Castle and the Suwa clan (Suwa Clan Castle Ruins)

    Uehara Castle in Chino was the base of the main Suwa clan line until Takeda Harunobu invaded Suwa in 1542 (Tenbun 11). It is designated, together with Kuwabara Castle in Suwa, as the Nagano Prefecture historic site 'Suwa Clan Castle Ruins'.

    TSN-HIST-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-001
    Grade A
    Suwa Taisha Kamisha Maemiya and the Suiga Spring

    Maemiya, a Kamisha shrine of Suwa Taisha in Miyagawa, Chino, stands on an alluvial fan above the Suiga spring, beside the site of the residence of the Ohori priest (a Nagano prefectural historic site). The shrine states that its present main hall was built in 1932 with old timber from the Ise Grand Shrine's periodic rebuilding.

    TSN-FAITH-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-002
    Grade A
    Kamisha Onbashira Yamadashi (Okoyayama, Tsunaokiba, Kiotoshi, Kawagoshi)

    In the 2022 Onbashira festival, eight fir logs for the Kamisha shrines were felled in October 2021 at the shrine-owned forest of Okoyayama in Chino, the first time in about 30 years that this forest supplied the logs. The Kamisha yamadashi was held on 2 April 2022 with the logs moved by vehicle in a single day.

    TSN-FAITH-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-003
    Grade A
    Moto-Misayama Site on Kirigamine

    The Moto-Misayama site on the Kirigamine plateau, south of the Yashimagahara wetland, is a medieval ritual site where the Misayama festival of the Suwa Taisha Shimosha was held until the early Edo period; terraces survive on the slope. A Waseda University team excavated it three times (dated by the museum to the Showa 30s) and found celadon and unglazed pottery.

    TSN-FAITH-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-004
    Grade A
    Mt. Tateshina and Tateshina Shrine (summit inner shrine and village shrine)

    Mt. Tateshina, at the northern end of the Yatsugatake range, straddles Chino City and Tateshina Town and carries the inner shrine (okusha) of Tateshina Shrine on its summit. The village shrine (satomiya) stands at its foot in Tateshina Town, and the shrine's tradition says the deity Takeinoebisu climbed the mountain after yielding the Suwa region to the Suwa deity (Wikipedia names Takeminakata).

    TSN-FAITH-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-005
    Grade A
    Gozaishi Shrine and the Doburoku Festival

    Gozaishi Shrine in Chino enshrines Koshinunakawahime, the mother goddess of the Suwa deity, and holds a festival on 27 April at which unrefined sake brewed in the shrine grounds is offered and shared. Because the deity is female, the shrine is said to rebuild its torii gate rather than erect the onbashira pillars.

    TSN-FAITH-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-FAITH-006
    Grade A
    Yumichi Kaido and its Kannon Statues

    Yumichi Kaido is the nickname of a prefectural road in Chino that leads toward the Okutateshina hot springs, lined with 66 Kannon statues, 33 on each of the two routes to Shibu Onsen and Meiji Onsen. The inn Meiji Onsen states that the statues were donated in the late Edo period and the mid-Meiji era; no official designation or builders' record was found.

    TSN-FAITH-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-001
    Grade A
    Yasujiro Ozu, Kogo Noda and Tateshina (Unkoso and Mugeiso villas)

    From 1954 the director Yasujiro Ozu worked with the screenwriter Kogo Noda in the Tateshina highlands; Ozu named his rented villa Mugeiso, and Chino City sources count six or seven screenplays written there. In 2003, for the centenary of his birth, Mugeiso was moved to Pool-daira, and the Ozu Memorial Tateshina Highland Film Festival has run since 1998.

    TSN-LIT-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-002
    Grade A
    Ito Sachio, Shinohara Shizuji and Tateshina (Tateshinasan-ka and the Araragi school)

    The Araragi poet Ito Sachio, introduced to Tateshina by the local poet Shinohara Shizuji, is said to have composed ten poems titled Tateshinasan-ka in 1909; a scroll of that title was designated a Chino City tangible cultural property in 1983. A stone monument to Sachio stands in Kita-yama, Tateshina, said to date from 1940.

    TSN-LIT-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-003
    Grade A
    Nitta Jiro, Kiri no Shisontachi (Descendants of the Mist)

    Kiri no Shisontachi is a novel by Nitta Jiro, born in Suwa, about local people opposing a toll road across the Kirigamine highlands. The Bunshun paperback appeared in March 1978; Wikipedia's bibliography gives a 1970 first edition from Bungeishunju, which could not be confirmed in NDL Search.

    TSN-LIT-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-004
    Grade S
    Jinchokan Moriya Historical Museum and Terunobu Fujimori's tea houses (Takasugi-an, Flying Mud Boat, Hikusugi-an)

    Opened in 1991 in Chino, the Jinchokan Moriya Historical Museum houses 1,618 documents of the Moriya family and is described by the Agency for Cultural Affairs as Terunobu Fujimori's first work. Three of his tea houses stand nearby; Takasugi-an is reported to be privately owned and closed to visitors except on tours.

    TSN-LIT-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-005
    Grade A
    Chino Cultural Complex and Chino City Museum of Art

    The Chino Cultural Complex, directly connected to Chino Station and housing the Chino City Museum of Art, opened on 1 October 2005. Designed by Yoshiaki Furuya, it was planned through citizen workshops from 1999 and received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize for architectural design in 2007.

    TSN-LIT-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-LIT-006
    Grade S
    Yatsugatake Museum of Art (Hara Village History and Folklore Museum)

    Opened in 1980 as a village-run museum after the sculptor Shimizu Takashi donated works, the Yatsugatake Museum of Art was designed by Togo Murano with a row of dome roofs. It also shows Jomon artifacts excavated in Hara Village.

    TSN-LIT-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-ROAD-001
    Grade A
    Kanazawa-juku (Koshu Kaido post town; moved from Aoyagi-juku; honjin site)

    Kanazawa-juku in Chino was a post town on the Koshu Kaido and the next stop after Tsutaki-juku; Wikipedia counts it as the 43rd from Edo, though the station numbering varies by source. It is said to have been moved in 1651 from the earlier Aoyagi-juku to its present site.

    TSN-ROAD-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-ROAD-002
    Grade A
    Daimon Pass and the Daimon Kaido

    Daimon Pass (1,441 m), just north of Lake Shirakaba on the Chino–Nagawa border, is the high point of the Daimon Kaido, now carried by National Route 152. The Yamanashi prefectural history-road site lists a route from Wakamiko via Tachisawa to Daimon Pass as a leading hypothesis for the Shingen Bodo, but this is an inference.

    TSN-ROAD-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-ROAD-003
    Grade A
    Tsuetsuki Pass and the Tsuetsuki Kaido

    Tsuetsuki Pass (1,247 m) on the Chino–Ina border carries National Route 152 and lies on the road between Takato and the Suwa Taisha Kamisha. The pass is said to be named after a deity who first planted his staff there, and a trailhead for Mt. Moriya is near the original pass.

    TSN-ROAD-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-ROAD-004
    Grade A
    Chino Station and the Chuo Main Line

    Chino Station opened on 25 November 1905 with the Fujimi–Okaya section of the Chuo Main Line. Wikipedia says the station name came from the Chino district of Miyagawa village; the municipality became Chino Town in 1948 and Chino City in 1958, and, per Wikipedia, agar-agar raw material and products were handled in volume until freight service ended in 1984.

    TSN-ROAD-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-001
    Grade A
    Sakamoto Yosen and the Kurikoshi-segi (carry-over weir) system, Chino

    Sakamoto Yosen (1736-1809), a village headman of Tazawa, devised the 'kurikoshi-segi' system, which links rivers by channels to pass surplus water southward to water-short land. After a petition in 1775 went unheeded, the first weir was cut in 1785, and 15 weirs are recorded by 1800, irrigating about 300 ha of new paddy.

    TSN-IND-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-002
    Grade S
    Takinoyu-segi and Ogawara-segi, World Heritage Irrigation Structures

    Takinoyu-segi (opened 1785) and Ogawara-segi (1792) in Chino take water from the Takinoyu River and were registered as World Heritage Irrigation Structures by ICID in November 2016, the first in Nagano Prefecture. Both are still maintained by local land improvement districts.

    TSN-IND-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-003
    Grade A
    Mishakaike Pond (agricultural reservoir, 1933)

    Mishakaike was completed in 1933 as an irrigation reservoir to dilute and warm the cold, strongly acidic water of the Shibu River for paddy fields, and was selected among Japan's 100 Best Reservoirs in March 2010. Higashiyama Kaii's painting 'Midori hibiku' (1982) is described in an essay in a Nagano Prefectural Museum of Art exhibition publication as depicting it.

    TSN-IND-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-004
    Grade A
    Warm-water reservoirs: Lake Shirakaba (Tateshina Oike) and Lake Tateshina

    Lake Shirakaba (completed 1946, first named Tateshina Oike) and Lake Tateshina (1952, per the prefecture) were built as 'warm-water reservoirs' that hold cold mountain water so it warms before reaching rice paddies. A cylindrical flow divider below Lake Tateshina splits the water 9:1 between two old weirs.

    TSN-IND-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-005
    Grade A
    Kaku-kanten (natural square agar) of Chino and Suwa

    Nagano Prefecture describes the Suwa area as Japan's only producer of 'kaku-kanten' (square-bar natural agar), introduced in the Tenpo era (1830-44) as a winter sideline of farmers. Where and when exactly it arrived differs by source, and in 2024 Nagano held 60.1% of national agar shipment value.

    TSN-IND-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-006
    Grade A
    Shimi (freeze-dried) foods of Chino: koridofu and winter preservation

    In Chino, 'shimi' foods such as tofu, daikon and mochi are hung outdoors to freeze at night and thaw by day for long keeping. The prefecture's tourism site says freeze-dried tofu took root there in the Taisho era and, according to a December 2025 article, only two traditional makers remain.

    TSN-IND-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-007
    Grade A
    Tateshina Onsen-kyo: history of the hot-spring baths (Taki-no-yu, Shin-yu, Kosai-no-yu, Shibu-no-yu)

    The Takashima domain is said to have owned the springs and left their running to contractors; in the late Meiji period they were mainly self-catering bathing places for nearby farmers, and in 1903 and 1909 they were transferred to village property wards (zaisanku) that let the baths to bidders. Origin tales (Sakanoue no Tamuramaro, Takeda Shingen) are legends; the earliest figures found are from a village record of 1879.

    TSN-IND-007 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-008
    Grade S
    Property wards (zaisanku) and villa-land development on the Tateshina plateau

    In Chino, village-level property wards that held former common forest leased land to developers (a paper dates the period to 1955-73), and villa estates grew on that leased land. The ward is a legal entity under Article 294 of the Local Autonomy Act, and Fujimi's records also mention a Fujimi property ward.

    TSN-IND-008 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-009
    Grade S
    Lake Shirakaba: an irrigation pond and its tourism use (Kashiwabara property ward, Ikenotaira land improvement district)

    Lake Shirakaba was completed in November 1946 as a prefectural irrigation pond that Kashiwabara residents had proposed and part-funded. Tourism began under the residents and the Kashiwabara property ward, and newcomers joined from about 1955.

    TSN-IND-009 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-010
    Grade S
    Venus Line (Tateshina and Kirigamine toll roads) and the nature-protection movement

    The Venus Line is the nickname for the Tateshina and Kirigamine toll roads built by the Nagano Prefectural Enterprise Bureau from 1961. Wikipedia records a protection movement against the Utsukushigahara section in 1971, and the toll roads were made free in 1986 and 2002.

    TSN-IND-010 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-011
    Grade S
    Kita-Yatsugatake Ropeway and Tsuboniwa

    The ropeway opened on 9 July 1967 as a prefectural enterprise and was transferred to a third-sector company in 1985. The operator says the Tsuboniwa lava plateau, which a paper dates to an eruption about 600 years ago, lies at the summit station.

    TSN-IND-011 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-IND-012
    Grade A
    Shinshu Nokogiri (Shinshu hand saws)

    Shinshu saws are hand-forged saws made mainly in Chino, Hara and Fujimi, designated a Nagano Prefecture traditional craft on 13 October 1983. They are said to originate from a saw smith, Fujii Jinkuro, invited from Edo by the Takashima domain during the Bunka era.

    TSN-IND-012 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-001
    Grade S
    Yokodake (Kita-Yokodake): the active volcano at the northern end of the Yatsugatake chain, by the JMA's description

    Yokodake, commonly called Kita-Yokodake, is a small volcano of lava flows and a lava dome at the northern end of the Yatsugatake chain, as the Japan Meteorological Agency describes it, added to Japan's list of active volcanoes in 2003. The Japan Meteorological Agency dates its latest lava-producing eruption (the Happyodaira lava) to about 600-800 years ago, while the Geological Survey of Japan's dataset gives about 2,250 years for the same lava.

    TSN-NAT-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-002
    Grade S
    Shimagare (wave regeneration) of subalpine firs on Mt. Shimagare

    Shimagare is a pattern in which bands of Veitch's and Maries' fir die along the contour lines and the bands creep uphill as saplings grow beneath them; Mt. Shimagare in the northern Yatsugatake is the best-known site. Wind is regarded as the leading explanation, but the cause is not settled: soil conditions and bark beetles with associated fungi have also been discussed.

    TSN-NAT-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-003
    Grade S
    Yashimagahara Wetland (Kirigamine Wetland Plant Community) and its peat

    Yashimagahara is a raised bog at about 1,630 m straddling Suwa and Shimosuwa, and part of the Kirigamine Wetland Plant Community, a national natural monument designated on 10 June 1960. Its peat is described as about 8 m thick and roughly 10,000 to 12,000 years old, depending on the source.

    TSN-NAT-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-004
    Grade A
    The semi-natural grasslands of Kirigamine (hay-cutting, controlled burning, day-lilies and deer)

    The grasslands of Kirigamine were kept open by village hay-cutting and burning until cutting largely stopped around the mid-1950s to early 1960s, after which woody plants spread and many Hemerocallis colonies disappeared. Deer fences were installed from 2010-2011, and controlled burns in 2005 and later included an escaped fire in April 2013 that burned about 220 ha.

    TSN-NAT-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-005
    Grade A
    Larch forests on the western foot of the Yatsugatake: native stands and plantation history

    Japanese larch is native to the high mountains of central Honshu and has been planted in Nagano since the early modern period, expanding greatly after the war. For the south-western foot of the Yatsugatake (Chino, Hara, Fujimi), a research report finds that grassland used for hay and fertilizer was planted with larch and red pine from the Meiji era onward, but the planters and dates are not documented in the sources found.

    TSN-NAT-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-006
    Grade S
    Shirakoma-no-ike pond and the moss forest of Kita-Yatsugatake (one of Japan's Precious Moss Forests)

    Shirakoma-no-ike is a pond at about 2,115 m in the northern Yatsugatake, on the border of Sakuho and Koumi towns. The primeval forest around it was certified by the Bryological Society of Japan on 30 August 2008 as one of Japan's Precious Moss Forests.

    TSN-NAT-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • TSN-NAT-007
    Grade S
    Yatsugatake-Chushin Kogen Quasi-National Park

    The park was designated on 1 June 1964 and spans Nagano and Yamanashi prefectures, covering 39,857 ha according to the Ministry of the Environment. It joins the Yatsugatake range with the Chushin highlands such as Kirigamine and Utsukushigahara.

    TSN-NAT-007 (full text in Japanese)
