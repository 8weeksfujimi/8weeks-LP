Suwa

54 records placed in Suwa. Municipalities used as the reference: Suwa City, Shimosuwa Town, Okaya City.

  • SUW-JOMON-001
    Grade S
    Hoshigatō Obsidian Source Site

    A Jomon obsidian-mining site on the east slope of Mount Hoshigatō, in the northwestern part of the Kirigamine massif in Shimosuwa, at about 1,500 m, with 193 mining pits recorded over roughly 35,000 square metres. It was designated a National Historic Site on 10 March 2015.

    SUW-JOMON-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-JOMON-002
    Grade A
    Sone Site (Lake Suwa underwater site)

    A Jomon Incipient-period site lying on the bed of Lake Suwa, reported by Suwa City as Japan's first underwater site, found in 1908 when a teacher sounding the lake brought up two arrowheads. Why it lies underwater led to the long Sone debate, and Fujimori Eiichi proposed in 1963 that lake levels had changed.

    SUW-JOMON-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-JOMON-003
    Grade S
    Nashikubo Site

    A Jomon settlement site on a small alluvial fan northwest of Lake Suwa in Okaya, the type site of the Nashikubo-style pottery of the early Middle Jomon. It was designated a National Historic Site on 11 January 1984, with 94 pit dwellings confirmed.

    SUW-JOMON-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-JOMON-004
    Grade S
    Kaido Site and the Jomon Deep Pot with a Face-Shaped Handle

    A Middle Jomon deep pot with a face-shaped handle, excavated at the Kaido site in Okaya, was designated an Important Cultural Property on 12 June 1989 and is held by the Okaya Art and Archaeological Museum. The face looks outward, and a snake-like motif is thought to appear at the opposite end of the rim.

    SUW-JOMON-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-JOMON-005
    Grade A
    Fune Kofun and Aozuka Kofun

    Fune Kofun in Suwa, from the early to middle fifth century, is described as the first tomb of the Suwa area and yielded two serpentine-bladed swords. Aozuka Kofun in Shimosuwa, designated a Prefectural Historic Site in 1965, is described by the town as a kind of keyhole-shaped tomb, the only one in the Suwa area.

    SUW-JOMON-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-JOMON-006
    Grade A
    Fujimori Eiichi and Amateur Archaeology in Suwa

    Fujimori Eiichi (1911-1973) was an archaeologist born in Kamisuwa who studied under Morimoto Rokuji and founded the Suwa Archaeological Institute after the war, remaining outside academia. About 60,000 artefacts from his collection became a nationally registered cultural property in 2012.

    SUW-JOMON-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-001
    Grade A
    Takashima Castle (the 'Floating Castle of Suwa')

    Takashima Castle on the shore of Lake Suwa was built by Hineno Takayoshi, a retainer of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and the city of Suwa dates its construction to 1598. Its keep was removed in 1875 and rebuilt in reinforced concrete in 1970.

    SUW-HIST-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-002
    Grade S
    Graves of the Suwa Lords of Takashima Domain

    The graves of the Suwa lords of Takashima Domain, at Onsenji in Suwa and Raigakuji in Chino, were designated a National Historic Site on 9 February 2017. The Suwa family ruled the domain from 1601 until the abolition of domains in 1871, and in 1781 the 6th lord retired to settle a retainers' dispute known as the Ninomaru affair.

    SUW-HIST-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-003
    Grade A
    The Battle of Wada Pass and the Rōnin Mound (Shimosuwa)

    On the 20th day of the 11th month of Genji 1 (18 December 1864 in the Gregorian calendar, according to Wikipedia) the Takashima and Matsumoto domains met a party of Mito loyalist rebels (the Tengutō) at Toyohashi near Wada Pass and were beaten. The town of Shimosuwa gives 14–15 rebel, 4 Matsumoto and 6 Takashima dead and designates the Rōnin Mound a town historic site.

    SUW-HIST-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-004
    Grade A
    Sagara Sōzō, the Sekihōtai and the Sakigake Mound (Shimosuwa)

    Sagara Sōzō and seven other officers of the Sekihōtai were beheaded at Shimosuwa on the 3rd day of the 3rd month of Keiō 4 (26 March 1868 in the Gregorian calendar, according to Wikipedia), and the Sakigake Mound was built there in 1870. The town of Shimosuwa designates it a historic site and records the posthumous ranks conferred on Sagara and Shibuya in 1928; the town describes the unit as having been branded a false imperial army and the executions as a wrongful conviction.

    SUW-HIST-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-005
    Grade A
    Matsudaira Tadateru and Kira Yoshichika, held in Takashima Castle

    Matsudaira Tadateru, a son of Tokugawa Ieyasu, was placed in the custody of Takashima Domain in 1626 and died in 1683 aged 92. Kira Yoshichika, heir of Kira Kozukenosuke, was held at the castle from 1703 and died in 1706 aged 21.

    SUW-HIST-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-006
    Grade A
    Former Watanabe Family Residence (Okaya)

    The Former Watanabe Family Residence in Okaya is the house of a dispersed samurai (sankyo bushi) family who served Takashima Domain and lived in the countryside; the city dates its founding to the mid-18th century. It was designated a Nagano Prefecture treasure on 12 August 1993, and three ministers came from the family.

    SUW-HIST-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-HIST-007
    Grade A
    Suwa Province (721-731) and the written forms of the name Suwa

    Suwa Province was split from Shinano Province in the sixth month of Yoro 5 (721) and merged back in the third month of Tenpyo 3 (731). The name is written Suwa (州羽) in the Kojiki and 諏方 in the Shoku Nihongi and Engishiki, and Wikipedia says the county's spelling was unified as 諏訪 in 1834.

    SUW-HIST-007 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-001
    Grade S
    Suwa Taisha Kamisha Honmiya (Upper Shrine Main Sanctuary)

    The Honmiya of Suwa Taisha's Upper Shrine in Suwa City has no main hall; its offering hall, worship hall and flanking halls, which the national explanation dates to a rebuilding in 1835, and an older four-legged gate said to date from 1608, are Important Cultural Properties. Whether the mountain behind it was long the object of worship is disputed.

    SUW-FAITH-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-002
    Grade A
    Kamisha Jinguji and the Separation of Shinto and Buddhism

    Beside the Upper Shrine's Honmiya stood a Buddhist temple complex with a Fugen hall and a 38 m five-storied pagoda; the pagoda was demolished in the winter of Meiji 1 (1868). Its images and ritual objects were dispersed to nearby temples, and in 2022 they were opened to the public at the same time at 25 temples and shrines and two museums.

    SUW-FAITH-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-003
    Grade A
    The Ohori and the Suwa Family (Ohori Residence at Miyatado)

    The Kamisha Ohori, a hereditary priest regarded as a living god, held the top post of the Suwa shrines until hereditary priesthoods were abolished after the Meiji Restoration. His residence stands in Nakasu, Suwa City; when the head of the family died in 2002, the city set up a fund to preserve and use the cultural properties.

    SUW-FAITH-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-004
    Grade A
    Omiwatari and Yatsurugi Shrine (the Rite of Observation and Its Records)

    When Lake Suwa freezes and the ice ridges up, Yatsurugi Shrine in Suwa City observes the ridges, judges them and divines the year; the ritual is a city-designated intangible folk cultural property. The last recognised omiwatari was in 2018, and a later compilation of notifications dating from 1443 survives in the Ohori family's papers.

    SUW-FAITH-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-005
    Grade A
    Tenaga Shrine and Ashinaga Shrine (the Hand-Long and Foot-Long Retainers)

    Tenaga Shrine on Chausuyama and Ashinaga Shrine in Shiga, both Suwa Taisha Upper Shrine auxiliary shrines in Suwa City, are said to honour the retainers Tenaga and Ashinaga of the god of Suwa. The Suwa City Museum records a tale of the two hunting fish together in Lake Suwa.

    SUW-FAITH-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-006
    Grade A
    The Seven Wonders of Suwa (Counts and Sources)

    The Seven Wonders of Suwa are counted differently by the shrine, the city museum and Wikipedia: the shrine's history page lists seven, the museum eight, and a Wikipedia entry counts 11 across the upper and lower shrines. A 1716 book already records them, but whether the framework goes back to medieval texts is unconfirmed.

    SUW-FAITH-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-007
    Grade S
    Suwa Taisha Shimosha: Akimiya and Harumiya (the two Heihaiden)

    The two halls of Suwa Taisha's lower shrine, Akimiya and Harumiya, were rebuilt in the Anei era (1772-1781) on the same plan by different carpenter lineages; both were designated Important Cultural Properties on 26 December 1983. The kami's spirit-seat still moves between them in February and August.

    SUW-FAITH-007 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-008
    Grade B
    Ofune Matsuri (Shimosha Summer Relocation Festival and the Brushwood Boat)

    On 1 August the spirit-seat of Suwa Taisha's lower shrine is carried from Harumiya to Akimiya, followed by a large boat of green brushwood bearing dolls of an old man and woman. The February relocation goes the other way.

    SUW-FAITH-008 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-009
    Grade A
    Tsutsugayu Shinji (Reed-Tube Gruel Divination at Harumiya)

    On the night of 14 January into the morning of the 15th, 44 reed tubes are boiled with rice and azuki beans in a cauldron at Harumiya, and the gruel inside them is read to forecast 43 crops and the state of the world. The shrine counts it among the Seven Wonders of Suwa.

    SUW-FAITH-009 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-010
    Grade A
    Shimosha Onbashira: Kiotoshi Slope and Shimekake

    In the lower-shrine Onbashira, eight logs are hauled from the Tanakiba to a steep slope of about 100 m and a maximum 35 degrees, then rest at Shimekake until the village-haul in May. The festival, held in Tiger and Monkey years, is a Nagano Prefecture intangible folk cultural property.

    SUW-FAITH-010 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-011
    Grade A
    Manji no Sekibutsu (Manji Stone Buddha)

    A large boulder carved as Amida Buddha, inscribed with the date 1 November of Manji 3 (1660), stands in Higashiyamada, Shimosuwa. Tradition says the stonemason's chisel drew blood while he cut stone for the Harumiya torii; the figure is a Shimosuwa town designated cultural property.

    SUW-FAITH-011 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-FAITH-012
    Grade B
    Moriya Shrine and Fujishima Shrines (the Two Banks of the Tenryu River)

    Moriya Shrine in Okaya and a Fujishima shrine across the Tenryu River are described, in Wikipedia, as commonly regarded as the camp sites of the native god Moriya and the incoming Suwa deity. Medieval texts place the contest at the foot of Mt Moriya, and the riverside tradition is recorded in Edo-period accounts.

    SUW-FAITH-012 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-001
    Grade S
    Shimaki Akahiko and Shiin Sanbo (Shimosuwa)

    Shimaki Akahiko (real name Kubota Toshihiko, 1876-1926), a poet of the Araragi school, was born in the Suwa area; the birthplace was once disputed, and Miyagawa Yasuo concludes it was Kami-Suwa and that he grew up in Toyohira Village (now Chino). His thatched former house in Takagi, Shimosuwa, which he seems to have named Shiin Sanbo around 1919-20, is a town-designated cultural property (1982).

    SUW-LIT-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-002
    Grade S
    Iwanami Shigeo and the Shinshu Fuju Bunko (Suwa)

    The Shinshu Fuju Bunko in Nakasu, Suwa, began in May 1947 when Iwanami Shoten promised to donate all its publications to young people of Nakasu village and gave the first 201 volumes. The city says it holds all Iwanami books published since 1947, and the library is named after a motto of the publisher's founder, Iwanami Shigeo (1881-1946), who is said to have been born in the village.

    SUW-LIT-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-003
    Grade A
    Fujiwhara Sakuhei, meteorologist, and the sky of Kirigamine

    Fujiwhara Sakuhei (1884-1950, born in Kami-Suwa) became the fifth director of the Central Meteorological Observatory in 1941. In 1932 he founded the Kirigamine Glider Club, and in 1934 held Japan's first glider meet at Kirigamine.

    SUW-LIT-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-004
    Grade A
    Kawai Sora (Sora's travel diary and his graves in Iki and Suwa)

    Kawai Sora, born in 1649 in Shimokuwabara village below Takashima castle (now Suwa), accompanied Matsuo Basho on the journey of the Narrow Road to the Interior and left the travel diary 'Sora Tabi Nikki', which the city of Iki describes as an Important Cultural Property. He died on Iki in 1710 while attending an official inspection tour, and has a grave at Noman-ji on Iki and a grave marker at Shoganji in Suwa.

    SUW-LIT-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-005
    Grade A
    Takei Takeo and the ILF Douga Museum of Art (Okaya)

    Takei Takeo (1894-1983), born in Hirano village (now Okaya), used the word 'doga' for pictures for children and made a total of 139 limited-edition 'kanpon' books. The ILF Douga Museum of Art is said to have opened in Okaya in 1998.

    SUW-LIT-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-LIT-006
    Grade S
    The Tatekawa and Osumi schools of Suwa temple carpenters

    Tatekawa Washiro Tomimune (said to have lived 1744-1807), a carpenter-sculptor of the Suwa Tatekawa school, is the builder named by Shimosuwa town for the Akimiya heihaiden of Suwa Taisha's Lower Shrine (Important Cultural Property, 1983). The town says the carpenter Murata Chozaemon Tomoshige of the Osumi school (the school's name is written in two ways in the sources) built the Harumiya hall to the same drawings.

    SUW-LIT-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-ROAD-001
    Grade S
    Shimosuwa-juku (Nakasendo post town where the Koshu Kaido joins)

    Shimosuwa-juku was the 29th post town on the Nakasendo from Edo, between the Wada and Shiojiri passes, and is described as the terminus of the Koshu Kaido. The honjin of the Iwanami family was designated a Nagano Prefecture treasure on 16 March 2023.

    SUW-ROAD-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-ROAD-002
    Grade A
    Kamisuwa-juku (Koshu Kaido post town under Takashima Castle)

    Kamisuwa-juku was a Koshu Kaido post station in the castle town of Takashima, 1 ri 11 cho from Shimosuwa-juku and 3 ri 14 cho from Kanazawa-juku. The Suwa City Museum says it had no honjin or waki-honjin, only a toiya, while Wikipedia lists one honjin.

    SUW-ROAD-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-ROAD-003
    Grade S
    Wada Pass (highest point of the Nakasendo)

    Wada Pass on the border of Shimosuwa and Nagawa is 1,531 m at the present pass, while the Edo-period Nakasendo crossed about 1 km to the west, at 1,615 m according to the Nipponica encyclopedia. The Wada-Shimosuwa stage was 5 ri 18 cho, a long haul described as the Nakasendo's most difficult stretch, and the Nakasendo was designated a national historic site on 3 October 1987.

    SUW-ROAD-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-ROAD-004
    Grade A
    Shiojiri Pass and the Imai Okoyasumi-honjin (Imai family residence)

    Shiojiri Pass on the Okaya-Shiojiri border carried the old Nakasendo; reference works give 1,055 m for the old Nakasendo pass and 999 m or 1,012 m for the present Route 20 pass. At Imai in Okaya, the Imai family residence (Imai Okoyasumi-honjin) is listed among Nagano's nationally registered tangible cultural properties.

    SUW-ROAD-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-ROAD-005
    Grade A
    The Chuo Main Line at Suwa (1905 opening, the Tatsuno detour, Enrei Tunnel)

    The Chuo Main Line reached Okaya from Fujimi on 25 November 1905, opening Kamisuwa, Shimosuwa and Okaya stations, and was extended to Shiojiri via Tatsuno on 11 June 1906. The Enrei Tunnel line of 1983 cut the Okaya-Shiojiri section to about 12 km.

    SUW-ROAD-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-001
    Grade A
    The Suwa-style Silk Reeling Machine and Okaya's Silk-Reeling Industry

    Sources say people in Okaya improved Italian and French reeling machinery in the early Meiji period into the Suwa-style reeler, which spread nationwide. Sources do not agree on the year of its development, and no official production figures were confirmed.

    SUW-IND-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-002
    Grade S
    Katakura-gumi and Katakura Kanetaro (Kaito Silk Mill, the former Katakura-gumi Office)

    Katakura Industries dates its silk reeling to 1873 in Kawagishi, Okaya, a Western-style Kaito mill in 1878, and Katakura-gumi in 1895. It merged the Tomioka Silk Mill in 1939 and donated its buildings to Tomioka City in 2005; the 1910 Katakura-gumi office is a Registered Tangible Cultural Property.

    SUW-IND-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-003
    Grade S
    The Former Hayashi Residence (Okaya)

    The residence was built by Hayashi Kunizo, a silk-reeling industrialist of modern Okaya, mostly around 1907, and is designated an Important Cultural Property (2002). The agency's description singles out the detached building's second floor, finished with gold-embossed leather paper.

    SUW-IND-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-004
    Grade S
    The Former Yamaichi Bayashi-gumi Silk Office and the 1927 Yamaichi Bayashi-gumi Dispute

    The two-storey office (1921) and gatehouse of the Yamaichi Bayashi-gumi silk company in Okaya are Registered Tangible Cultural Properties (2005). From 30 August to 17 September 1927 a labour dispute at the company took place, described in a 2024 paper as the largest prewar silk-reeling dispute in Japan; the same paper says the strikers' demands were not met.

    SUW-IND-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-005
    Grade A
    Okaya Silk Museum (Silk Fact Okaya)

    The municipal museum opened in October 1964 and moved in August 2014 to the former Okaya Silk-Reeling Experiment Station site, with a working silk mill inside. It holds a French-style reeling machine from the Tomioka Silk Mill, said to be the only one left, and eight reelers registered as Mechanical Engineering Heritage.

    SUW-IND-005 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-006
    Grade A
    Maruyama Tank and Water for the Silk Mills

    The Maruyama Tank is a circular brick water-storage structure in Okaya that Nagano Prefecture describes as completed in 1914 and listed as Modern Industrial Heritage. A magazine article says a water association of silk mills pumped water from the Tenryu River to it.

    SUW-IND-006 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-007
    Grade A
    Kamisuwa Onsen and the Geyser (Unified Hot Spring Sources, Nanatsugama)

    Kamisuwa on the east shore of Lake Suwa had nearly 500 spring outlets by 1926; the city later merged ten sources into one supply system, completed in 1987. A geyser burst out during drilling in 1983 and reached about 50 m according to a magazine-type article; a 2017 article says it was driven by a compressor, and its present operation is unconfirmed.

    SUW-IND-007 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-008
    Grade S
    Katakurakan (Bathhouse, Hall and Connecting Corridor; Important Cultural Property)

    Katakurakan, a bathing and social facility on the Lake Suwa shore, was completed in 1928 to a design by Moriyama Matsunosuke, and its bathhouse, hall and connecting corridor were designated Important Cultural Properties on 20 June 2011. The foundation that runs it states that the second Katakura Kanetaro conceived it after a 1922-23 tour of the Americas and Europe.

    SUW-IND-008 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-009
    Grade A
    Shimosuwa Onsen (Wata-no-Yu, Tanga-no-Yu and Koyu)

    The earliest surviving record of Shimosuwa's hot springs is said to be a poem written by the monk Issan Ichinei in 1300. The town began its municipal hot-spring supply project in fiscal 1985 and carried out construction until fiscal 1995.

    SUW-IND-009 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-010
    Grade A
    Suwa Sake Breweries and Kyokai No. 7 Yeast

    In 1946 a yeast strain was isolated from a brewery in Suwa; Wikipedia says it has been distributed by the Brewing Society of Japan as Kyokai yeast strain 7 since that fiscal year. Five breweries in Kamisuwa are presented by the Japan National Tourism Organization as 500 meters from the Koshu Kaido.

    SUW-IND-010 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-011
    Grade A
    Genealogy of Suwa's Precision Industry (Daiwa Kogyo, Suwa Seikosha, Sankyo Seiki; Watches and Music Boxes)

    Epson states that its predecessor Daiwa Kogyo was founded in May 1942. The quartz wristwatch Seiko Astron 35SQ, developed at Suwa Seikosha, was introduced in Tokyo on 25 December 1969.

    SUW-IND-011 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-012
    Grade S
    Pond Smelt (Wakasagi) and Fishing on Lake Suwa

    Pond smelt eggs were transplanted into Lake Suwa from Lake Kasumigaura in 1915 and 1916, according to a 2007 prefectural fisheries review, which adds that eggs were by then shipped nationwide. Dead smelt, carp and crucian carp were found on the morning of 26 July 2016; 2,531 kg were collected on 28, 30 and 31 July along the lake's northern shore.

    SUW-IND-012 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-IND-013
    Grade S
    Geta Skates and the Ice of Lake Suwa

    Geta skates, wooden clogs fitted with iron blades, are described in the national registration of the 'Suwa Geta Skate Collection' (130 items, registered 22 March 2023) as invented by a Shimosuwa metalworker in the Meiji era. A prefectural document dates the start of skating around Lake Suwa to 1903 and the first lake-circuit race to 1909, while a tourism article gives 1908.

    SUW-IND-013 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-NAT-001
    Grade S
    The Origin of Lake Suwa (Itoigawa–Shizuoka Tectonic Line and the Suwa Basin)

    Lake Suwa lies at 759 m above sea level, covers 13.3 km2 and is at most 7.2 m deep, with 31 inflowing rivers and a single outflow, the Tenryu River. The Suwa basin is thought to be a pull-apart basin formed by lateral slip along the Itoigawa–Shizuoka Tectonic Line fault zone.

    SUW-NAT-001 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-NAT-002
    Grade S
    Reading the Omiwatari Records as a Climate Archive

    Freeze dates of Lake Suwa kept since 1443 were compared with break-up dates of Finland's Torne River in a 2016 Scientific Reports paper by Sharma and colleagues, which reported later freezing in Suwa since the Industrial Revolution. The authors set aside 1682-1923 as unreliable because of calendar changes and missing dates.

    SUW-NAT-002 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-NAT-003
    Grade S
    Water Quality of Lake Suwa and the Course of Its Cleanup

    Algal blooms became conspicuous in Lake Suwa in 1965; a basin-wide sewerage system began service in 1979 and the lake was designated under the Lake Law in 1986. Recent prefectural material reports higher transparency but COD and total nitrogen still above the standards, and water chestnut (hishi) covering about 13 percent of the lake.

    SUW-NAT-003 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-NAT-004
    Grade S
    Kamaguchi Sluice Gate, Where the Tenryu River Leaves Lake Suwa

    Kamaguchi Sluice Gate in Okaya is the only outlet of Lake Suwa, where the Tenryu River (main-stem length 213 km) leaves the lake. The first gate was completed in 1936 (one prefectural document gives 1937) with a maximum discharge of 200 m3/s; the second, completed in 1988, is rated for 600 m3/s but has been operated at lower limits since 1992.

    SUW-NAT-004 (full text in Japanese)
  • SUW-NAT-005
    Grade S
    Enrei Kotori-no-Mori (Bird Forest of the Enrei Highlands)

    The woods around Enrei Onodachi Park in Okaya are designated a 'bird forest', and a prefectural document of fiscal 2025 says the 'bird bus' began in 1954 and has run for 72 years. The birdsong is among the Ministry of the Environment's 100 Soundscapes of Japan; the special protection area lies within Yatsugatake-Chushin Kogen Quasi-National Park, while the separate Enrei-Ojo Prefectural Natural Park was designated on 25 June 1964.

    SUW-NAT-005 (full text in Japanese)
