Method and Legend

方法と凡例

How this notebook and ledger are written, and what they have decided not to write. This page sets out how confidence is shown, how sources are handled, the writer's position, and the rules for citation and correction. Please read it before using a record.

01 · ScopeWhat this is, and what it is not

What is here is a collection of fragments of publicly available material about the land, arranged with a degree of confidence and a source attached to each. It is not an encyclopedia, a travel guide or a scholarly monograph. It is offered as a first lead, so that you can go to the sources yourself and check against the land.

Nor is it a route guide. Conditions on mountains, lakes, passes and winter roads change with the season and the weather. Before you go, check with official information for that day.

The method of this archive is not to delete what is uncertain, but to keep it as uncertain and to note how uncertain it is.

Kinds of pages

Notebook — NotesFirst-person notes on travel and daily life. Written only from the writer's own experience.
Notebook — PlacesRecords of places. Short rewrites of ledger records, addressed to people who visit the place.
LedgerThe body of the records, corresponding to the drafts behind the notebook. For each record it publishes the text, the confidence grades, the verification notes, the sources and the production log.
Record pagesPages that open one ledger record at a URL that does not change. This is the unit to use when citing. From the index of records you can reach every record (English version).
Entrances to the landsPages of contents for each land (Fujimi, Tateshina, Suwa). All three have the same form and are generated mechanically from the ledger.
Inn notes and writer's notesNotes attached to a record. They describe how a visitor might take that record; they are not verified fact. In records for Fujimi they are called “inn notes”; in records for Tateshina and Suwa, where there is no inn, they are called “writer's notes”.
Graph and mapPages that view the same ledger by the links between records (graph) and by position on the land (map).

Ground pages and margin pages

Every ledger record carries one of two labels. A ground page is a record based mainly on firm sources. A margin page is a record about tradition, peripheral accounts, or the very fact that “no record survives”. Margin pages are not lesser than ground pages. The shape of a land is made both of what was written down and of what was not.

02 · One ledger, three landsOne ledger, three lands

There is one ledger. Its records are divided by land into three. Each record has one principal land.

Fujimi
富士見		The southwestern foot of the Yatsugatake mountains, on the border of Kai and Shinano. It covers Fujimi town and subjects that extend beyond the town. The town is where the inn is; publication began in July 2026.
Tateshina
蓼科		The western and northern foot and highlands of the Yatsugatake. Centred on Chino city and extending into Hara village, it covers Tateshina, Lake Shirakaba, Kirigamine and the Kita-Yatsugatake. Added in November 2026.
Suwa
諏訪		The area around Lake Suwa. It covers the lake, towns and passes of Suwa city, Shimosuwa town and Okaya city. Added in November 2026.

“Suwa” here means the three cities and towns around Lake Suwa. It does not mean the wider “Suwa region”, which also takes in Fujimi town, Chino city and Hara village.

The lands were added because, as the Fujimi records were dug into, the sources kept pointing outside the town. Sakamoto Yōsen, said to have been involved in the Otokoto weir in Fujimi, was a man of Chino. Miyasaka Eiichi, said to have directed the first excavation at Idojiri, was the man who dug Togariishi. On the Kōshū Kaidō, the post town after Tsutaki is Kanazawa in Chino, and the one after that is Kami-Suwa. Some of the rain that falls on the passes of Fujimi flows down the Miya River into Lake Suwa. The Fujimi ledger has, from the start, a record of the rites at the Honmiya of Suwa Taisha Kamisha; and in the account of the precision industry once called the “Switzerland of the East”, the subject was Suwa city, Okaya city and Shimosuwa town around the lake. The same names of people, the same water, the same festivals and the same roads continued across administrative boundaries. The three lands are less the records of separate towns than one mountain foot and the lake where its waters gather, written from three directions.

How the lines are drawn

  • Standard. A record is placed in the land of the municipality where the centre of its subject lies. Fujimi is Fujimi town; Tateshina is Chino city and Hara village; Suwa is Suwa city, Shimosuwa town and Okaya city. This allocation decides most of the records.
  • Subjects that cross lines. A subject that crosses a municipal boundary is placed in one land, with the other noted as a “related land”. For Kamisha, the Honmiya (Suwa city) is written under Suwa and the Maemiya (Chino city) under Tateshina. The burial ground of the lords of Takashima lies in two places, in Suwa city and Chino city, and is placed in Suwa.
  • Records placed differently from the standard. Records about the Kirigamine highland itself (Yashimagahara wetland, the former Mi-sayama site, the grassland) touch Suwa city and Shimosuwa town by municipality, but are placed in Tateshina. This is because the highland was written first as one land continuing from Tateshina and Lake Shirakaba. By contrast, the obsidian site at Hoshigatō, though in the same Kirigamine massif, lies in Shimosuwa town and has been studied by the town, so it is placed in Suwa. Other ways of placing these are possible.
  • Labels for related lands. When the municipality of a record also falls within a municipality of another land, a dotted label such as “+Suwa” is attached. The label is attached mechanically from the municipality's name. When the ledger is filtered by land, records bearing the label appear as well.
  • Hara village. Because it lies between Fujimi and Tateshina, it is counted as a municipality of both lands.
  • The lands are not of equal size. Fujimi is one town, Tateshina is the name of a highland, and Suwa is a lake and three cities and towns. The division is not meant for comparing the number of records between lands. If the lines were drawn differently, the numbers would change.
  • When the lines are changed, this will be written on this page with the date. On record pages, links to records in other lands are shown as “neighbouring lands”.

The Fujimi records include, from the start, some that extend outside the town (Chino city, Hara village, Hokuto city, Suwa city and others). The label “includes areas outside the town” marks them. Fujimi records do not yet have a municipality field, so no dotted label is attached to them. Where a subject overlaps with a record in another land, the two are made to point to each other rather than repeat each other.

03 · Confidence gradesFive confidence grades

Every record carries a degree of confidence (a grade). The grade is not a score for the record as a whole. It is the confidence of each claim within one record. A notation such as “A/C” shows that solid accounts and tradition sit together in the same record. Which claim has which grade is written in each record's “breakdown of confidence (verification notes)”.

GradeNameWhen it is given
SEstablishedWhat was confirmed directly in primary or official material such as official national or prefectural databases, public notices, and peer-reviewed papers.
ASolidWhat is stated in official material of a municipality or museum; or where several independent, reliable sources agree.
BContestedWhere accounts differ; where only one source could be found; or where the account comes from an interested party (a company, facility, tourism association, shrine or temple, and the inn).
CTraditionOral accounts, origin stories, and what is “said to be”.
DHearsay, to be verifiedAccounts that could not be corroborated. They are not written in the notebook. In the ledger they are either kept to the verification notes or written with the fact that they are hearsay stated plainly.

The lower the confidence, the weaker the assertion in the text. Phrases such as “is considered”, “is said to” and “it is told that” are not ornament; they are the display of the grade.

These five grades are a yardstick that this archive decided on for itself. They are not grades set by a learned society or a public body. So please do not trust the grade letter alone. A grade has meaning only together with the basis written in the verification notes — which statement in which source it was confirmed against. Writing so that the basis can be traced, publishing where the material comes from, and setting out other theories and local tradition side by side are practices learned from the norms listed under Intellectual debts.

A grade is a judgement about the reliability of the material, not about the value of the event. Tradition (C) is not “below” solid record (A); it is a different kind of record.

04 · SourcesHandling of sources

  • Only sources that were actually opened during verification, and in which the fact to be written was confirmed to appear, are listed as sources.
  • Every record has two or more sources. Public and near-primary material is looked for first; if none is found, that is written in the verification notes.
  • Encyclopedias are used as an entry point. The records for Tateshina and Suwa were verified under a rule that S or A is not given on the basis of Wikipedia alone. Every record containing S or A has at least one source that is official material, a cultural-property database, a museum or a paper (this is checked by machine). However, in some places a claim was given A solely because several publisher encyclopedias (such as the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho and the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei) agree. Which claims these are is written in the verification notes, by the name of the encyclopedia. The Fujimi records still contain places where a statement from an encyclopedia was given A. Where they are is written in the verification notes, and they will be reviewed in turn.
  • What a party has said about itself — a company, facility, tourism association, shrine or temple — is treated as grade B. It is not discarded; it is read as a record of “the party says this”. Our own communications as an inn are treated the same way.
  • We do not write as if we had read books or papers that we have not read. Where a basic work on a subject was confirmed to exist but its text has not been read, it is listed under “Further reading” on the record page, clearly marked “not seen”, and is not used as a basis for the text.
  • Links will break eventually. If you find one that has, please tell us.

Labels for types of source

On record pages, each source carries a label for its type. There are ten: cultural-property database / national and local government / papers, reports and bibliographies / museums and cultural bodies / newspapers and magazines / local publications / encyclopedias / parties and tourism communications / personal records / unclassified. In the records for Tateshina and Suwa, the verifier assigned the label to each source; in the Fujimi records, they are classified mechanically from the domain of the URL. The classification rules are published as a configuration file.

A type label is not a guarantee of confidence. A personal record may be a primary observation that only someone who went to the place could write; a public page may still carry an old theory. Always read the verification notes for confidence.

05 · ProcessProcess, and the use of machines

  1. Excavation — Decide the subject and gather publicly available material.
  2. Verification — Open the sources, check claim by claim, and propose confidence grades. Discrepancies between sources are also recorded.
  3. Compilation — Write the text. For claims of low confidence, weaken the assertion.
  4. Audit — A separate pair of eyes checks the sources against the text again. Remarks and corrections are kept in the “production log” of each record.
  5. Decision to publish — Decide to publish, not to publish, or to withhold.

Steps one to four use conversational AI. One AI actually opens and reads the pages of the sources, and another AI audits that work. The decision in step five, and any decision of “this is not to be written”, is made by a person.

From the Suwa records a step was added. For each main fact, the verifier extracts the relevant passage of the source page verbatim and keeps it, and the auditor checks it against the source. A fact that cannot be extracted this way is not written in the text. The extracts themselves are copies of the sources' wording and are therefore not published.

The verification notes and production logs keep the working vocabulary as it was. The “lead writer” is the role that reads the whole of a record and decides corrections (this too is an AI; the decision to publish or not is made by a person). “Leads” are preliminary information the lead writer raised before verification began; a lead that could not be confirmed in the sources is not written in the text, and the verification notes say so. Where a source is referred to by a two-character abbreviation, what it refers to is stated at its first appearance.

AI can write plausible errors. A verifier can also claim more confidence than it has confirmed. So the range that was checked is kept in the verification notes, and the remarks and corrections in the production log, and all of it is published. The workings are shown not to ask for trust, but so that the work can be doubted.

The Notes in the notebook (first-person notes) are not written by AI, because only the person who had the experience can write them.

06 · What we leave outWhat we have decided to leave out

  • Evaluation or criticism of companies, facilities and organisations now operating, or of people now living.
  • Promotional language. The inn too is not written about in the text of the records.
  • Stories of progress such as “it used to be backward and has now improved”. Praise for development or for attracting investment. Lament for what has been lost. The course of events is only set out in the order in which it happened.
  • Anything that identifies a living private individual.
  • Subjects that call for consideration of living people concerned. These are recorded in-house as history, but publication is withheld.
  • Stories that risk becoming edifying anecdotes. We wait to write them until the corroboration is complete.
  • Practical information that changes, such as opening hours and prices.

What we have decided to write instead

  • Gaps in the record. We write openly what we searched for and did not find, and that material does not survive. That no record survives and that nothing happened are not the same.
  • Where accounts differ, we set them side by side without leaning to either.
  • Numbers are written as the source gives them, and where sources differ, they are written as differing.

07 · Position and interestsThe writer's position

This notebook and ledger are made by 8weeks, which runs an inn in Fujimi town. People whose business depends on people visiting the land are writing about that land. This is an interest. Please discount accordingly when you read.

So that this interest does not bend the records, we have decided the following.

  • The inn is not written about in the text or headings of records.
  • A pointer to the inn is placed in one place only, outside the record pages, alongside the disclosure of this position.
  • Our own communications are also treated as “a party's communications” (grade B).
  • The records are published free of charge and carry no advertising.
  • We place no devices that follow readers. As of November 2026, no analytics tags are present on these pages.

On Tateshina and Suwa. As of November 2026, 8weeks has no inn in Tateshina or Chino, nor around Lake Suwa. The records of these two lands are placed not as guidance to an inn but as records of the same mountain foot and the lake. Not only for inns: if any relationship arises, such as a contract, an investment or an ongoing business, it will be added here with its date.

Another bias should also be written down. The writer is someone who moved to this land. The writer cannot write with the eyes of a person who has lived here since birth. What an outsider could see is written as an outsider.

08 · LimitationsLimitations and biases

  • It leans on material that can be read on the web. Much printed material — town, city and district histories, old municipal bulletins, bound volumes of newspapers — has not yet been read.
  • No interviews have been conducted. The voices of people of the land are limited to what someone has already written down and published.
  • Much has not been checked on the ground. Some coordinates on the map are approximate positions.
  • The subjects are uneven. Records of the Jōmon period and of belief are thick; records of daily life, work, women and people who moved in remain thin. The unevenness in what material survives is reflected here as it is.
  • The thickness differs by land. Fujimi, Tateshina and Suwa differ in the number of records, in the depth of digging and in when they were made. Fujimi records do not yet have a municipality field or a field for “what has not yet been confirmed”. Their source types, too, are not assigned by a verifier but classified mechanically from the domain.
  • Places that relied on encyclopedias remain. The proportion is shown under The ledger in numbers.
  • Machine errors are possible. Even with repeated audits, not all of them are caught.
  • It is almost entirely in Japanese. English is limited to the added notes in the notebook and to the headings and summaries of the records for Tateshina and Suwa.

Rules for when interviews begin

The largest gap is that we have not heard directly from people of the land. When interviews are conducted, the following steps will be kept. None has yet been conducted, so this is a promise, not a track record.

  • We first explain what the interview is for, and where and in what form it will be published.
  • The person may decline, stop partway, or withdraw it afterwards.
  • Whether to give or withhold the person's name or the name of their hamlet is decided in discussion with them. We also tell them beforehand that, even if withheld, it may still be possible to tell who is meant.
  • What is written is shown to the person before it is published.

09 · Citing, reuse, correctionsCiting, reuse, corrections

Citing

The URL of each record page does not change. Every page has a citation format and a button to copy it. The format is as follows (the Japanese format is shown).

8weeks Local Archive「記録の見出し」『8weeks Local Archive 台帳』記録のID，確度，版の日付．記録の頁のURL（参照した日付）

When a record is corrected, the date of its version changes. When you cite, please always consult the record's sources as well, not only this ledger. This ledger is a signpost to the sources.

Reuse

You may refer to and quote freely if you state the source (8weeks Local Archive and each record's references). The ledger data itself is also published: the ledger (JSON; the division into lands is in each record's subregion field) · classification and land configuration · statistics.

Corrections

Please send pointers to errors, additional sources and memories of the land through Instagram. Corrections are not made silently. What was corrected and how is kept in each record's “production log” and in the history below.

This method is not something we devised. We list the norms we read when putting it together, and the works from which we borrowed ways of asking questions. In keeping with the rule of this page (do not write as if we had read what we have not), we have added, one by one, how far each was read.

12Summary

What this is. A single sourced ledger of the land from the western foot of the Yatsugatake mountains down to Lake Suwa in Nagano, Japan. Its records are divided into three lands: Fujimi, Tateshina, and Suwa — here meaning the city and towns around the lake (Suwa, Shimosuwa and Okaya), not the wider Suwa district. It is neither an encyclopedia nor a travel guide: it gathers fragments of what is publicly documented, grades how certain each claim is, and points to where the evidence comes from, so that you can check for yourself.

Confidence grades. Every record carries a grade, applied claim by claim rather than to the record as a whole. S established (confirmed directly in official databases, public notices or peer-reviewed work) · A solid (official municipal or museum sources, or several independent sources in agreement) · B contested (accounts differ, a single source, or an interested party speaking about itself) · C tradition (oral accounts and origin stories) · D unverified hearsay. A grade such as “A/C” means that solid facts and tradition sit side by side in one record. The Japanese grade letters are kept as they are in every language.

Sources. A source is listed only if it was actually opened and found to state the fact. Every record has at least two. Encyclopedias are used as entry points. Statements by companies, facilities, tourism bodies and shrines about themselves are treated as grade B — including our own.

How it is made. Research, verification, drafting and auditing are done with conversational AI: one model opens and reads the sources, another audits the result, and the exchange is published with each record. Decisions to publish, withhold or wait are made by people. AI can be confidently wrong, which is why the apparatus is left visible.

Who is writing. This archive is made by 8weeks, which runs an inn in Fujimi. People whose livelihood depends on visitors are writing about the place visitors come to; please read with that in mind. The text of the records says nothing about the inn; the only pointer to it sits in one place outside the record pages, next to this disclosure; the records are free and carry no advertising. As of November 2026 we operate no accommodation in Tateshina or around Lake Suwa; if any contract, investment or ongoing business relationship arises there, it will be added here with its date.

Limits. The archive leans on what can be read online; most printed local histories remain unread, no interviews have been conducted, and much has not been checked on the ground. It is thick on prehistory and belief and thin on everyday life, labour and women. Almost all of it is in Japanese.

Citing and correcting. Each record has a permanent page with a citation format. You may quote and reuse the records with attribution; please also consult the sources each record cites. Corrections are welcome and are never made silently — what was changed is kept with the record.

Notice — This archive gathers fragments confirmed from publicly available material, as far as they could be confirmed, and does not guarantee the accuracy of its contents. For anything that matters, please check the sources and the place itself. The rules written on this page are also still unfinished; if you notice anything, please tell us. For the inn, see here.
